Brief 01 Before the campaigns How the machine worked
1655

England takes Jamaica

The new colony is exposed, underfunded, and planted inside Spain’s Caribbean system. Port Royal grows from that insecurity.

Political cover

Modyford’s commissions

Governor Thomas Modyford turns privateers into an unofficial navy: cheaper than defending Jamaica and useful against Spain.

Terms of the trade

Buccaneer, privateer, pirate

A commission could make a raid legal to England and criminal to Spain. “Buccaneer” described the Caribbean brotherhood; “pirate” was often the verdict of the losing side.

The target system

Spain’s long supply lines

Ports, silver roads, and distant garrisons made an enormous empire rich but brittle. Morgan attacked connections and chokepoints, not territory he meant to hold.

Reader’s lens

Strategy and terror were inseparable

Ransom, forced guides, torture, imprisonment, and threats against civilians were operating methods—not incidental excesses. Accounts disagree on particulars, but not on the campaigns’ coercive foundation.

Brief 03 After the raids 1672–1692
1672

Called to England

Panama has become a diplomatic liability. Morgan is sent across the Atlantic to answer for the raid.

1674

Sir Henry returns

Instead of disgrace, Morgan is knighted and returns as lieutenant governor of Jamaica—the outlaw absorbed into empire.

1684–85

The battle over memory

An English edition of Exquemelin depicts Morgan as a pirate and monster. Morgan sues the publishers; the contested claims are never tested at trial.

1688

Morgan dies

Ill health ends a career spent moving between sanctioned violence, wealth, and colonial office.

1692

Port Royal sinks

Earthquake, liquefaction, and sea destroy much of the town. The catastrophe supplies the book’s final judgment on the world that made Morgan.

This is a reading aid, not a tactical reconstruction. Historic sites are plotted near their modern locations and river-crossing waypoints are approximate. Exquemelin is indispensable but contested; English and Spanish records conflict over dates, numbers, atrocities, and responsibility for the fire at Panama.
